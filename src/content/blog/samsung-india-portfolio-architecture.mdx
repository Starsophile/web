## The question

A smartphone portfolio can cover many price points and still confuse buyers if neighbouring models appear to offer similar value. The important question is not simply how many models a brand sells. It is whether each model has a clear role in the portfolio.

This is an independent research project about Samsung India's mid-range portfolio. It is not an exposé or a claim that overlap alone causes weak sales.

## What I am examining

- **Price-band coverage:** where models sit and how close their effective prices are after offers.
- **SKU and specification overlap:** whether nearby models communicate distinct reasons to buy.
- **Channel positioning:** how online-first and offline-led models are presented to different buyers.
- **Consumer-facing differentiation:** whether a shopper can quickly explain why one model is worth choosing over another.

## Why it matters

When product propositions blur together, consumers may struggle to understand the upgrade path. Retail staff may also find it harder to explain the difference quickly. But overlap can be intentional: shared hardware, channel-specific offers, and different retail economics may serve strategic purposes.

The analysis therefore needs to distinguish **visible similarity** from **commercially unnecessary duplication**.

## How I would evaluate it

1. Build a model-by-model matrix with launch price, current street price, chipset, display, battery, camera, update policy and channel availability.
2. Compare each model against its nearest alternatives, not only against the brand's full lineup.
3. Check whether differences are meaningful to buyers in that price band.
4. Test the initial hypothesis against sales, sell-through, channel feedback or consumer research wherever those data are available.

## Current limitation

Public specifications and advertised prices can reveal positioning, but they cannot independently prove cannibalisation, profitability, or the internal rationale for a launch. Those conclusions require stronger commercial evidence.

## The product-strategy implication

A useful portfolio is not necessarily the one with the fewest devices. It is the one where each product has a legible target customer, a clear reason to exist, and a defensible place in the price ladder.

*Independent student research. Findings should be treated as hypotheses until validated against primary market and channel data.*
